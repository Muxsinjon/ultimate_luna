# NASA Space Apps 2026 submission kit

The rules below were read from the official [Project Submission Guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/) (page dated 2026-11-13) on 2026-11-15. Re-check the page before submitting; this file is not the official source.

## Rules that affect this project

- **Deadline:** **Sunday 15 November 2026, 23:59 local time of the team's Local Event**. For the Universal Event, use the Team Owner's local time. Only the last saved submission is judged.
- **Required fields:**
  - project name;
  - high-level summary;
  - link to the project demo;
  - link to the final project;
  - detailed description;
  - use of Super Intelligence (SI);
  - NASA data;
  - Space Agency Partner data and other references.
- **Demo:** a video of **30 seconds or less**, or a slide deck of **at most 7 slides**, on a public link that needs no login. It must be in English (or carry English subtitles). Teams that exceed the limits are not eligible for Global Judging.
- **NASA data:** at least one NASA data source or resource is required for a Global Award.
- **SI (AI) use:**
  - It is permitted, but not for coming up with the idea of how to solve the challenge.
  - Disclose where and how it was used: code and data in descriptive text **and metadata**; images and videos with a visible watermark.
  - Generated content must not contain NASA branding (logos, flags, mission identifiers).
- **People:** no images, video or audio of anyone under 18.
- **Before submitting:** check "Terms and Conditions" and "Confirm All Work is Original", then **Submit for Judging**.

## Draft field text (edit names and team details)

**Project name:** LunaWindow: Lunar Landing Site Navigator

**High-level summary:**
- **What it is:** LunaWindow answers where, when and why to land near the lunar south pole.
- **Choosing sites:** users compare two real sites on NASA's 5 m terrain, with slope, roughness and hazards.
- **Timing:** they see when the Sun and Earth are actually visible above the real terrain horizon, using JPL Horizons geometry with libration and LOLA horizons.
- **Planning:** they get explainable mission-date rankings under their own constraints.
- **Experiencing:** they can stand on the surface at that place and time.
- **Trust:** every number cites its NASA source, units and limits.

**Final project link:** https://github.com/Muxsinjon/new_luna (repository). The GitHub Pages deployment is disabled pending the team's decision; see README "Deploy".

**Detailed description:** use the README sections "Workspaces", "How it works" and "Validation", plus [MILESTONE9_ACCEPTANCE.md](MILESTONE9_ACCEPTANCE.md).
- **Tools:** TypeScript, React, Three.js, Vite and Vitest; Python with numpy, rasterio and scipy; Playwright.

**Use of SI:**
- **What it was used for:** an AI coding assistant (Anthropic Claude) helped write and test code, data-processing scripts and documentation, under the team's direction. The team chose the idea and approach.
- **Where the numbers come from:** every scientific value comes from NASA/JPL data processed by the documented pipeline. Values are validated against independent references (JPL Horizons, native NASA GeoTIFFs), not generated by AI.
- **Where it is disclosed:** [AI_USAGE.md](AI_USAGE.md), code metadata and export metadata.

**NASA data:** see the table in [DATA_SOURCES.md](DATA_SOURCES.md).

**Space Agency Partner data and other references:**
- JAXA Kaguya (SELENE) laser altimetry underlies the Diviner PRP mesh (Paige et al. 2010). Mission coordinates come from ISRO (Chandrayaan-3) and JAXA (SLIM), as cited in `pipeline/sites.py`.
- **Open-source libraries:** React, Three.js, Vite, numpy, rasterio, pyproj, scipy, Playwright.
- **Fonts:** Barlow, under the OFL.
